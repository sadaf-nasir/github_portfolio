1 Abstract

This study explores the predictors of customer satisfaction as measured by Net Promoter Scores (NPS) using a random forest regression approach applied to a dataset of fictional bank complaints. The analysis identified Days_To_Resolve as the most important predictor, highlighting the central role of timely resolution in shaping customer perceptions. Although the correlation between resolution time and NPS was modest, its prominence across models underscores its practical significance. Among categorical variables, Language emerged as the most influential, with French-language complaints associated with slightly lower NPS. These findings suggest that operational improvements that shorten complaint resolution times may meaningfully boost customer satisfaction and, in turn, drive business growth. Future work will incorporate sentiment analysis of complaint text to further enhance predictive accuracy.

2 Introduction

In an increasingly customer-driven marketplace, understanding and predicting consumer sentiment has become a cornerstone of effective marketing strategy. Among the many metrics used to gauge customer experience, the Net Promoter Score (NPS) has emerged as one of the most influential. Introduced by Fred Reichheld in the Harvard Business Review in 2003, NPS simplifies customer feedback into a single question, “How likely are you to recommend our company to a friend or colleague?” and transforms the answer into a powerful signal of loyalty, advocacy, and business potential (Reichheld, 2003). The score, calculated by subtracting the percentage of detractors (those giving a score of 0–6) from the percentage of promoters (scores of 9–10), provides a clear, interpretable indicator of customer satisfaction that has been widely adopted across industries.

What sets NPS apart from traditional customer satisfaction measures is its strong correlation with long-term financial performance. According to Reichheld and other researchers, companies with higher NPS tend to outperform their competitors in areas such as growth, retention, and profitability (Reichheld, 2003; Markey, Reichheld & Dullweber, 2020). This is because NPS captures not just satisfaction, but emotional commitment and future behavior, which are two factors critical to driving organic growth through word-of-mouth referrals. From a marketing perspective, this makes NPS both a diagnostic tool and a strategic compass. Businesses can segment their customer base, tailor messaging, refine service delivery, and even optimize product development by understanding which customers are promoters, passives, or detractors, and why. Furthermore, as consumers become more vocal and empowered through digital platforms, a company’s ability to convert detractors into promoters is more important than ever for maintaining brand reputation and competitive advantage.

By leveraging data science techniques to predict NPS outcomes, companies can move from reactive analysis to proactive strategy. Instead of waiting for negative feedback to surface, predictive modeling can identify pain points and opportunities in real time, allowing marketing and customer service teams to intervene earlier, personalize experiences, and build stronger customer relationships. This project is grounded in this exact philosophy: using data-driven insights to understand what drives customer loyalty and to help businesses use NPS not just as a metric, but as a catalyst for transformation.

This project seeks to build a random forest model that can accurately estimate a customer’s likelihood of promoting or detracting from a fictional bank, based on the characteristics of their complaint and how it was handled. By incorporating variables such as complaint category, origin, language, and time to resolution, this study aims to uncover actionable patterns that drive customer satisfaction and loyalty. Ultimately, this model offers a blueprint for businesses to better anticipate customer needs, prioritize service interventions, and strategically invest in the factors that truly influence NPS ratings. This project hopes to demonstrate how data science can serve as a critical lever for customer-focused marketing and long-term business growth.

3 Methods

3.1 Dataset

The dataset used in this study was obtained from a publicly accessible GitHub repository maintained by Adam Ross Nelson, J.D. Ph.D, a data science teaching professor at the University of Wisconsin - Madison (Nelson, 2024). It contains anonymized records of customer complaints submitted to a fictional bank. The dataset includes 10 variables and 398 observations, each representing a unique customer complaint. Key variables include the bank service associated with the complaint (Product), the language in which the complaint was written (Language), the communication channel used to initiate the complaint (Origin), and the number of days taken to resolve the complaint (Days_To_Resolve). Each observation also includes a free-text complaint (Complaint), a numeric Net Promoter Score (NPS Response) indicating customer satisfaction, and de-identified personal information such as Name and Address, which are not used in the present analysis. This dataset is designed for educational purposes and reflects realistic patterns in customer service interactions within the banking sector.

3.4 Random Forest Model

3.4.1 Random Forest with One-Hot Encoding

A random forest model was run in this project using OneHotEncoder(). The categorical predictors were all combined into one Python list for easier use later in the model. The outcome variable was defined as “NPS Response”. The numeric variable Days_To_Resolve was standardized using StandardScaler() from sklearn, ensuring it has a mean of 0 and standard deviation of 1, which helps the model treat it appropriately during training. Next, the categorical variables (“Language”, “Product”, and “Origin”) were one-hot encoded using OneHotEncoder, converting each category into binary columns so they can be used in the model. These processed numeric and categorical features were then concatenated into a single dataset X_1. The data were split into training and testing sets using a 67%-33% ratio. A random forest regression model (rf_1) was then trained on the training data. Finally, predictions were made on the test set, and the model’s performance was evaluated using Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE).

Code 
# random forest model with one-hot encoding

# define predictors
cat_preds = ['Language', 'Product', 'Origin']
y_1 = df['NPS Response']

# scale numeric feature
the_scaler = StandardScaler().set_output(transform="pandas")
X_num_1 = the_scaler.fit_transform(df[["Days_To_Resolve"]])

# One-hot encode categorical features
encoder = OneHotEncoder(sparse_output=False, handle_unknown="ignore")
x_cat_array_1 = encoder.fit_transform(df[cat_preds])
X_cat_1 = pd.DataFrame(x_cat_array_1,
                       columns=encoder.get_feature_names_out(cat_preds),
                       index=df.index)

# combine all features into X
X_1 = pd.concat([X_num_1, X_cat_1], axis=1)

# train-test split
X_train_1, X_test_1, y_train_1, y_test_1 = train_test_split(
    X_1, y_1,test_size=0.33, random_state=42)

# random Forest Regression
rf_1 = RandomForestRegressor(random_state=42)
rf_1.fit(X_train_1, y_train_1)

# RMSE
rmse_1 = np.sqrt(mean_squared_error(y_test_1, rf_1.predict(X_test_1)))
print("RMSE (Model 1):", rmse_1)

4 Results

4.1 Model Performance

The random forest model was evaluated based on its Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) on the test data. Using OneHotEncoder(), the model achieved an RMSE of 3.52. The bar plot visualizations make it easier to interpret which variables had the greatest impact on NPS prediction, clearly showing that the number of days taken to resolve complaint had the greatest influence on the customer’s reported net promoter score. Additionally, the barplot shows that “French” and “Spanish” complaints had the least influence in the model, which is consistent with there being only 16 observations in each response category.

4.2 Feature Importance

To identify the most influential predictors of NPS, feature importance values were extracted from the trained random forest model. Days_To_Resolve emerged as the most important predictor by a significant margin (see ?@fig-rf1). This result aligns with earlier findings from exploratory data analysis, which showed a small but meaningful negative correlation between resolution time and NPS response in ?@tbl-crr (r = -0.10). Shorter resolution times were associated with higher NPS scores, indicating that customers value swift complaint resolution.

Among the categorical features, Language had higher importance than Product and Origin, with complaints in French associated with lower NPS scores. However, their influence was substantially lower than that of Days_To_Resolve. Product and Origin had the lowest importance scores, reflecting their relatively uniform NPS distributions observed during exploratory visualization.

The consistent prominence of Days_To_Resolve in both models underscores the critical role of efficient issue resolution in shaping customer loyalty and advocacy. The feature importance analysis confirms that resolution speed is a key driver of customer satisfaction and should be prioritized in service improvement efforts.

5 Discussion

The goal of this analysis was to identify complaint characteristics that best predict customers’ Net Promoter Scores (NPS) in order to inform strategies for improving customer satisfaction. Using random forest regression, Days_To_Resolve was found to be the most important predictor of NPS, outperforming all categorical variables by a substantial margin. This finding supports the hypothesis that resolution speed is a critical componenet of customer sentiment.

The strong importance of Days_To_Resolve is consistent with prior research and business intuition: customers are more likely to rate their experience positively when their issues are resolved quickly. Although the correlation between Days_To_Resolve and NPS was modest (r = -0.10), the feature’s central role in the model emphasizes its practical relevance when considered alongside other predictors.

Among categorical features, Language had the next highest importance, with complaints in French associated with slightly lower NPS. While Product and Origin offered minimal additional predictive value, their inclusion may still help capture small subgroup differences or interaction effects in future modeling efforts.

Overall, this analysis underscores the business impact of timely resolution. Organizations seeking to improve customer satisfaction should consider investing in tools, staffing, or workflows that reduce the time needed to resolve complaints. Reducing resolution time may yield disproportionately positive effects on customer perception and loyalty, as reflected in higher NPS ratings.

To build on these findings, future work could explore incorporating sentiment analysis of the complaint text as a predictor of NPS. Sentiment scores may capture nuances in customer emotion that are not fully explained by structural features like resolution time or complaint category. This could enhance model performance and offer deeper insights into what drives customer loyalty.

In addition to sentiment, segmenting the data by customer type or complaint severity may uncover subgroup patterns that may not emerge in aggregate models, enabling more targeted strategies for improving satisfaction. Ultimately, improving customer satisfaction isn’t just about better service; it’s a core element of long-term business growth.
